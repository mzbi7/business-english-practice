Business English Grammar Radar

High-impact rules explicitly designed for native corporate meetings and communications.

Prepositions: IN vs ON vs AT

Think of an upside-down pyramid: general to specific.

IN (Broadest / Enclosed)

Time: in Q3, in 2026, in October, in the morning

Place: in the Chicago office, in a meeting room, in the tech sector

ON (Specific Days / Surfaces / Tech)

Time: on Tuesday, on October 27th, on schedule

Tech/Media: on Zoom, on Slack, on the call, on LinkedIn

AT (Pinpoint Exact Time / Points)

Time: at 9:30 AM, at noon, at the end of the day (EOD)

Place: at headquarters, at my desk, at the conference

💡 Pro tip: We say "in the meeting" (participating), but "on the call" (digital line)!

Articles in Corporate English

When do we say the data vs a data (never!)?

THE (Shared Specific Knowledge)

Use when both speaker and listener know exactly which item.

"Let's review the metrics from last sprint."

"I spoke with the hiring manager."

A / AN (One of many / First mention)

Single countable noun mentioned non-specifically.

"We need a contingency plan."

"She is an experienced director."

ZERO ARTICLE (∅) (General concepts & Uncountable)

DO NOT put 'the' before broad company concepts:

"We value ∅ feedback." (Not 'the feedback')

"I am at ∅ work." (Never 'at the work')

"Based on ∅ recent data..."

💡 Pro tip: Acronyms as concepts take zero: "We need to improve ROI" (not the ROI).

The Golden Time Clause Rule

The #1 ESL mistake in roadmap updates & deadlines.

THE GOLDEN FORMULA
[Time Marker] + [Present Tense], [will + Verb]

Time markers: When, As soon as, Once, Before, After, Until. NEVER put "will" inside the time clause!

Common Mistake:

"When we will sign the deal, we will celebrate."

Professional Standard:

"When we sign the deal, we will celebrate."

Another example:

"As soon as she arrives, I'll brief her."
